# Explore the crazy stuff; simplicity is earned

## In brief

Design is a cool profession—you get to create totally new interactions and define new ways of doing things. That excitement can overshadow the basic question of what people actually want, and it is easy to get lost in it. The answer is not to stop exploring: try the crazy stuff, show other people the crazy stuff, and let the final result—often really simple and really obvious—be earned by all of it.

## Exploration is the job, not a detour

Explore the wild ideas even when the conventional answer looks likely. A reliable sign of burnout is when the exploration stops—when the goal quietly becomes the least effort that gets an acceptable result. That framing belongs to the voice of the business, not the voice of the user.

## The simple result is the result of the exploration

The final interface might look really simple and really obvious. That simplicity is the product of everything that was tried and discarded—all the crazy ideas, all the effort. This holds even when the simple result is the first thing you tried: you still have to try the other things, and think through what merit each one has, to know the simple answer is the right one rather than the first one.

## Working rules

- Explore beyond the likely answer, including the crazy versions, and show them to people.
- Treat the end of exploration as a warning sign, not efficiency.
- Let simple, obvious solutions be conclusions of exploration, not substitutes for it.
- After each experiment, name what merit it has before moving on.
