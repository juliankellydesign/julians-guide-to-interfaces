# Use quotation marks, apostrophes, and primes correctly

## In brief

Use quotation marks for quotations, apostrophes for contractions and omissions, and prime marks for measurements. The glyph communicates the function. When correct curly punctuation cannot be guaranteed, a consistent straight or prime-like fallback is better than a curly mark facing the wrong direction.

## Use quotation marks for quotations

Quotation marks curve toward the quoted material. Opening and closing marks face opposite directions:

```text
“Double quotation”
‘Single quotation’
```

The opening marks are `“` and `‘`. The closing marks are `”` and `’`. Their exact drawings depend on the typeface, but each side should be identifiable by its direction.

## Use apostrophes for contractions and omissions

An apostrophe normally uses the same glyph as the closing single quotation mark: `’`.

Use it for contractions and possessives:

```text
don’t
Julian’s
```

Use the right apostrophe for a truncated year:

```text
’26
```

The apostrophe represents the omitted leading digits in `2026`. It is the trailing mark attached to the remaining year, not an opening quotation mark. Do not use `‘26`.

## Use primes for measurements

Prime marks are straighter than quotation marks and do not form an opening and closing pair. Use the prime `′` for feet or minutes and the double prime `″` for inches or seconds:

```text
5′ 11″
```

Do not substitute directional curly quotation marks when the content is a measurement.

## Prefer a neutral fallback to the wrong direction

Correct punctuation is the goal. If the system cannot reliably produce the correct quotation mark or apostrophe, I would rather use a consistent straight or prime-like mark than render a curly mark facing the wrong direction.

This is a fallback, not a reason to ignore the distinction when the correct glyphs are available.

## Working rules

- Use directional quotation marks around quotations.
- Use the right apostrophe `’` for contractions, possessives, and omitted characters.
- Use `’26`, not `‘26`, for a truncated year.
- Use prime `′` and double prime `″` marks for measurements.
- Prefer a consistent neutral mark over an incorrectly directed curly mark when correct punctuation cannot be guaranteed.
