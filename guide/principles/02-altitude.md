# Change your altitude

## In brief

When I approach designing an interface, I jump back and forth between a super narrow view of it and a super wide view of it. The ability to change the elevation of your viewpoint—how broadly you look at the thing—is one of the most important skills a designer can have. It matters most when you are designing something someone else asked for; with self-initiated ideas the framing arrives with the idea, but a brief from someone else has to be examined from above before it is built from below.

## Two altitudes, two modes

Low altitude is making. Stay close to the page, to the pixels and the metal. Apply rigor, but do not let every unresolved question stop the work—this is where things get tried.

High altitude is editing. Pull up and ask: does this make sense? Does this work? Is it actually centered on something users have said they want, a behavior observed in research, or a new way of doing things worth trusting your intuition on?

Then zoom back in, make the adjustments, and zoom out to judge again. The loop is the skill—neither altitude is home.

## Be harsh on the work, gentle on yourself

Critique mode should be harsh on the work. Making mode should be gentle on the maker. Making is hard and ideas are fragile; judged too early, at the wrong altitude, they die before they can be evaluated fairly.

## Working rules

- Alternate deliberately between the narrow view and the wide view; do not settle at either.
- Make at low altitude, close to the pixels; edit at high altitude, against what people actually want.
- Examine someone else's brief from high altitude before building it at low altitude.
- Be harsh on the work in critique mode and gentle on yourself in making mode.
