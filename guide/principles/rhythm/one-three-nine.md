# Think in 1-3-9

## In brief

I often think in a 1-3-9 ratio when I think about ratios and sizing. One dominant element, three supporting elements, nine detail elements—each level of hierarchy roughly three times more numerous, and individually smaller, than the level above. Treat the ratio as scaffolding: a starting structure for a composition, not a rule the finished layout must satisfy.

## The ratio sketches a hierarchy

Imagine one big heading, three smaller headings, and nine lines of body text. The tripling makes each level unmistakable: no reader confuses the one with the three, or the three with the nine. When levels are too close in count or in size, the hierarchy flattens and nothing leads.

The ratio is a compositional sketch, not a census. A page does not need exactly three subheadings and nine lines; it needs the proportional feeling that each level steps down decisively from the one above.

## It may also describe area

The same ratio can describe surface area: a dominant surface, supporting surfaces at around a third of its area, and detail elements at around a ninth. I have not settled how far this application extends—treat it as a lens to try against a layout, not as a rule.

## Start from it as scaffolding

Beginning a composition from unlimited options is slow. Like [personal taste as scaffolding](../interface/personal-taste-as-scaffolding.md), the ratio narrows the option space: propose one dominant element, three supporting, nine detail, and let the work develop from there. The structure gets a layout moving before the content is fully resolved.

Scaffolding yields. Keep the ratio while it helps the composition, and let the content reshape it—two supporting elements, or twelve lines—when the material requires a different structure. The ratio's job is to make the first decisions fast, not to survive to the final layout.

## Judge with it, do not compute with it

1-3-9 is a proportion to check a composition against, not a formula for deriving values. Exact type sizes come from the type scale, spacing from the spacing ladder, and surface dimensions from the content. Use the ratio to ask whether the rendered hierarchy steps down clearly enough, then adjust with the applicable system.

## Working rules

- Use 1-3-9 as scaffolding when structuring a hierarchy: start from one dominant element, three supporting, nine detail.
- Let the content reshape the ratio; the scaffold makes early decisions fast rather than constraining the final layout.
- Check a composition that feels flat or crowded against the ratio; its levels may be too close in count or size.
- Aim for the proportional feeling rather than exact counts.
- Treat the surface-area application as an exploratory lens rather than a settled rule.
- Derive exact values from the applicable system, not from the ratio.
