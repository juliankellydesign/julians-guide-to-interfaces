# Rhythm and swing

## In brief

Establish rhythm before introducing swing. Standardize baselines, grids, tokens, type sizes, spacing, and repeated structures as far as possible. Use swing when one purposeful exception is needed to interrupt monotony or restore attention.

## Establish rhythm first

Rhythm is the consistency that makes an interface legible. It gives the reader a pattern they can learn and reduces the number of new decisions they need to interpret.

Establish rhythm through:

- Shared baselines
- Layout grids
- Spacing tokens
- Type sizes and roles
- Repeated section structures
- Consistent component behavior

Standardize as far as possible before introducing variation. A clear system makes relationships easier to understand and makes any later exception visible.

## Use swing to interrupt monotony

People do not read an interface with constant attention. Repetition can become monotonous, causing important information to be overlooked. Swing is a purposeful change to the established rhythm that brings the reader’s attention back to the content.

Swing is functional. It can indicate that a section has greater importance, that the content mode has changed, or that the reader has reached a transition or conclusion.

Swing is not general variety. If every repeated item is different, there is no established rhythm to break.

## Four-section example

Assume a page contains four sections that could use the same format.

| Approach | Treatment | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No rhythm | Give all four sections different formats | The reader must interpret four systems |
| Rhythm only | Give all four sections the same format | The page is clear but may become monotonous |
| Rhythm with swing | Leave three sections uncontained and place one section in a card for a specific reason | The system remains legible and the contained section restores attention |

For example, keep three sections uncontained and put only the section containing a key decision inside a card. The container is the exception. It communicates that the section should be treated differently without giving every section a different format.

Swing may also use a different scale, alignment, spacing pattern, or composition. It may even break a convention established earlier in the page. The difference should be limited to what the section needs to communicate.

## Swing across systems

Swing is not limited to layout. It also applies to typography, color, and motion.

- A type size or alignment can move away from the calculated scale when the rendered hierarchy needs optical correction.
- A color can move away from the calculated curve when the perceived contrast is wrong on the actual display or within the surrounding palette.
- A keyframe can move away from an equal time subdivision when the animation needs a less mechanical rhythm.

Mathematical systems establish rhythm, but they are imprecise models of human perception. Question why an exception feels better, then preserve it when it communicates more effectively for people.

## Requirements for swing

Before breaking the rhythm, identify:

1. The established pattern.
2. The item that needs different treatment.
3. The reason the reader should notice it.
4. The smallest change that communicates that reason.

If there is no reason for the exception, keep the rhythm.
