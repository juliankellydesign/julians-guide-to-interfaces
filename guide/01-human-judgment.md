# Human judgment and mathematical systems

## In brief

Use grids, color math, type math, and timing systems to establish consistency and make decisions faster. Treat them as tools, not objective descriptions of reality. When something feels better outside the system, question that feeling but do not discard it. Design for people.

## Math is a model

Mathematical systems reduce complexity. A grid aligns elements. A type scale creates hierarchy. A color curve distributes lightness. A timing grid synchronizes motion.

These systems are useful because they are simplified. That also makes them imprecise representations of reality. They cannot fully model:

- Differences between displays
- Differences in human perception
- The way darker tones may separate differently from lighter tones
- Optical effects created by glyphs, shapes, and surrounding content
- Cultural context and learned conventions
- The attention and expectations a person brings to the interface

## Treat feeling as evidence

Sometimes a mathematically aligned result feels wrong and a small deviation feels better. The feeling may contain perceptual or cultural information that has not been expressed in the system.

Do not accept the feeling without question. Identify what changed, who benefits, and what the deviation communicates. Do not discard the feeling simply because it cannot be described by the current formula.

“Feels better” is a prompt to investigate.

## Deadness is a design failure

An interface can be internally consistent and still feel dead. This often happens when mathematical alignment is treated as the objective rather than the starting structure.

Consistency should make an interface easier to understand. When it removes emphasis, energy, or a necessary human cue, use swing to restore what the system removed.

## Swing across disciplines

Swing is a purposeful deviation from an established system. It applies beyond layout:

- **Typography:** Adjust a calculated size, weight, line height, or alignment when the rendered type needs optical correction or different emphasis.
- **Color:** Adjust a calculated tone when the perceived difference is too weak or too strong on the actual display or within the surrounding colors.
- **Layout:** Break a grid, spacing pattern, or container convention when the content needs a different relationship or renewed attention.
- **Motion:** Move selected keyframes away from equal subdivisions when the animation needs a less mechanical rhythm.

The deviation should remain limited and should have a reason.

## Working rules

- Establish the mathematical system first.
- Review the rendered result in its actual context.
- Treat discomfort or preference as evidence to investigate.
- Ask what the deviation communicates and who it helps.
- Test across relevant displays, content, and people when possible.
- Keep the exception limited so the underlying rhythm remains legible.
- Design for human perception rather than mathematical compliance.
