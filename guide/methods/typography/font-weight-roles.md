# Assign font weights by role

## In brief

Start body copy and content at regular. Use medium when a header or another role needs a subtle increase. Use semibold for interactive content. Use bold for content that needs strong attention. Adapt the exact increase to the design system, brand, and typeface.

## Default roles

| Weight | Starting role |
| --- | --- |
| Regular | Body copy and content |
| Medium | Headers and optical correction when regular looks too light |
| Semibold | Interactive content |
| Bold | Content that needs strong attention |

These are starting roles, not universal mappings. Weight names produce different visual results in different typefaces.

## Headers

Increase weight as text moves from body content into header territory. A shift from regular to medium may be enough to reinforce the hierarchy without changing the character of the type.

## Interactive content

Interactive content should be heavier than surrounding body content. This applies to button labels and to interactive text within a larger block.

- Button labels should always be at least medium.
- Interactive content usually begins at semibold.
- White labels on colored or dark button backgrounds should be at least semibold.

## Strong attention

Use bold for content that genuinely needs to call attention to itself. Do not use it as a default header weight when medium or semibold creates enough distinction.

## Working rules

- Begin body copy and content at regular.
- Increase headers to at least medium when regular does not separate them sufficiently.
- Keep button labels at medium or heavier.
- Use at least semibold for white button labels on colored or dark backgrounds.
- Use semibold as the usual starting point for interactive content.
- Reserve bold for content that needs strong attention.
- Preserve an existing weight system unless replacing it is part of the task.
