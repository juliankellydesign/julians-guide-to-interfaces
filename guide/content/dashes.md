# Use em dashes, en dashes, and hyphens by function

## In brief

Use an em dash to change direction inside a sentence, an en dash to communicate a range, and a hyphen to join parts of a word. Do not use a hyphen as a substitute for either dash.

## Use em dashes for a change in direction

An em dash `—` can function like a comma, colon, or parenthesis. It introduces a new direction inside a sentence without ending the original thought.

A second em dash can return the reader to the original sentence:

```text
The interface establishes a pattern—one the reader can learn quickly—before introducing an exception.
```

The first dash pushes into the interruption. The second dash pops back to the original direction of the sentence.

Use an em dash followed by a space to attribute a quotation:

```text
“Quotation text.”
— Author
```

## Use en dashes for ranges

An en dash `–` communicates a range between two values. Use thin spaces around it:

```text
8:00 AM – 6:00 PM
pages 24 – 32
```

The en dash means “through” or “to.” It connects the beginning and end of the range without changing the direction of the sentence.

## Use hyphens inside words

A hyphen `-` joins parts of a word. Use it for:

- Compound words
- Prefixes or suffixes that require a visible connection
- Hyphenation when a word must break across lines

Do not use a hyphen in place of an em dash or en dash.

Avoid automatic word hyphenation in interfaces. Interface copy is usually short enough to rewrite, resize, or reflow without breaking words across lines.

## Working rules

- Use em dashes for interruptions, changes in direction, and quotation attribution.
- Use a paired em dash to return from an interruption to the original sentence.
- Use en dashes with thin spaces for numeric, time, and page ranges.
- Use hyphens for compound words, affixes, and necessary word breaks.
- Do not substitute hyphens for em dashes or en dashes.
- Avoid word hyphenation in interfaces.
