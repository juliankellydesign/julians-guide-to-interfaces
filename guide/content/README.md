# Content

## In brief

Content should be direct and informative. It should help someone understand the interface, make a decision, or complete an action. Additional style is useful only when it communicates something.

## Content is design

I treat content the same way I treat every other part of an interface. Every word should serve a purpose.

Before adding content, identify its job. Common jobs include:

- Naming an object or action
- Explaining what happened
- Setting an expectation
- Helping someone make a decision
- Telling someone what to do next
- Describing an error and how to recover from it

If a piece of content has no job, remove it.

## Be direct

State the relevant information without making the reader interpret decorative language. Prefer specific verbs and concrete nouns. Put the information needed for the next decision first.

Direct does not mean cold or uniform. The amount of explanation and the tone should match the situation. A destructive action requires precision. An onboarding moment may need reassurance. Both should remain clear about what is happening.

## Use style to communicate

Style is justified when it changes or improves the meaning. For example:

- Tone can establish the relationship between the product and the user.
- Emphasis can show which information is most important.
- A metaphor can make an unfamiliar concept easier to understand.
- Humor can reduce tension when it does not obscure the next action.
- An aside can use a more personal or reflective tone because its job is to communicate context rather than instruction.

Do not add personality, cleverness, or variation by default. Use them when they perform a specific communication job.

## Use capitalization and punctuation consistently

Choose a capitalization and punctuation scheme and apply it consistently throughout the interface. A change in either should communicate a change in meaning or function.

Punctuation is part of the interaction language. It can complete a statement, indicate that an action has more steps, or frame a route to help. Do not add it as decoration.

When establishing or changing an interface writing system, use [`../methods/content/capitalization-and-punctuation.md`](../methods/content/capitalization-and-punctuation.md) for my default scheme.

## Use each dash by function

Use em dashes for changes in direction, en dashes for ranges, and hyphens inside words. Do not use a hyphen as a substitute for either dash, and avoid word hyphenation in interfaces. See [`dashes.md`](dashes.md) for the full principle.

## Working rules

- Identify the purpose of the content before writing it.
- Put the information needed for the next action first.
- Use specific language and remove unnecessary qualifiers.
- Match tone to the context and consequence of the interaction.
- Keep style only when it adds meaning.
- Apply capitalization and punctuation consistently.
- Use punctuation to communicate function.
- Use em dashes, en dashes, and hyphens according to their distinct functions.
- Avoid word hyphenation in interfaces.
- Read the content in the interface, not only in a document.
